# Research Report

**Query:** Is chain-of-thought prompting an effective reasoning strategy for LLMs, or does it primarily improve output formatting? The literature disagrees—find the real fault lines and explain what accounts for the conflicting results.

## Summary

Research covered 5 of 5 sub-questions across 12 claim groups. 2 claim group(s) have contradicting evidence — see Conflicts Surfaced.

## Findings

### What empirical evidence distinguishes chain-of-thought prompting from answer-based prompting in improving LLM reasoning on math and logic benchmarks?

**Claim:** Chain-of-thought prompting improves LLM performance on reasoning-based tasks [1]

- Confidence: low | Status: weak

- Scope: when using few-shot learning


**Claim:** There exist empirical results that appear to show chain-of-thought can improve quality [3]

- Confidence: low | Status: disputed

- **Note:** This claim has contradicting evidence.


### How does model scale interact with chain-of-thought effectiveness—is CoT primarily beneficial for large language models above a certain parameter threshold? [adversarial]

**Claim:** Chain-of-thought prompting enables reasoning abilities to emerge naturally in sufficiently large language models. [5]

- Confidence: low | Status: weak

- Scope: in sufficiently large language models


**Claim:** Such reasoning abilities emerge naturally in sufficiently large language models via a simple method called chain-of-thought prompting. [9]

- Confidence: low | Status: weak

- Scope: in sufficiently large language models


### What role does benchmark contamination and dataset overlap play in explaining chain-of-thought performance gains in published studies? [adversarial]

- Confidence: low | Status: weak

- Scope: based on empirical results


### When does chain-of-thought prompting fail to improve LLM performance on reasoning tasks despite appearing to show intermediate steps? [adversarial]

- Confidence: low | Status: disputed
