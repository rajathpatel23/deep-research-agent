Summary

Research suggests that inference-time compute scaling can improve LLM reasoning on certain tasks, as evidenced by OpenAI O3 achieving 87.5% on ARC-AGI at substantial cost and observed linear accuracy scaling with log-compute for some domains. However, the evidence base is limited—findings primarily come from small-scale studies across nine models and eight tasks, and whether scratchpad lengthening and other scaling strategies effective for mathematical reasoning generalize to broader reasoning remains unclear. Direct comparisons between different compute allocation strategies (chain-of-thought, best-of-N, tree search) and documented failure cases or diminishing returns are largely uncovered by available evidence.

    Research Report

    Query: What is the current state of inference-time compute scaling for LLM reasoning? Separate what has been empirically validated from what is still speculative, and identify where the evidence is too thin to draw conclusions.

    Summary

    Research indicates that inference-time compute scaling—using methods like Best-of-N, beam search, and tree search—can measurably improve LLM reasoning on math, coding, and STEM tasks without requiring additional training, with models like OpenAI o1 and O3 showing substantial gains; however, the evidence base is thin and largely derived from proprietary systems with limited public disclosure of methods. Studies suggest the effectiveness of test-time scaling varies considerably depending on task difficulty, and crucially, this approach currently shows little benefit for knowledge-intensive tasks where factual retrieval matters more than step-by-step reasoning. Pre-training scaling is reportedly reaching diminishing returns, which has increased interest in inference-time approaches, though whether these gains will generalize broadly or sustain further scaling remains speculative given the weak evidence quality and high computational costs (e.g., OpenAI O3 reportedly exceeding $1,000 per task on ARC-AGI).
